# TerrainFly — Product Idea & Technical Architecture

## 1. Product Overview

TerrainFly is a web-based application that transforms GPS activities into immersive 3D terrain visualizations and cinematic route flyover videos.

Users can upload Garmin FIT/GPX activity files and automatically generate realistic 3D representations of their running, hiking, trekking, cycling, or walking routes.

The primary goal is to make it effortless to turn an outdoor activity into a visually appealing, shareable video, similar to Strava's 3D Flyover feature but with greater customization and cinematic control.

## 2. Core User Experience

1. User uploads a Garmin FIT, GPX, or TCX file.
2. Application extracts GPS coordinates, elevation, timestamps, distance, and available activity statistics.
3. System retrieves 3D terrain elevation data and map imagery for the activity's geographical area.
4. Recorded GPS route is rendered over the 3D landscape.
5. User can interact with the map through zoom, rotation, and tilt.
6. Application generates an animated flyover following the recorded route.
7. User customizes camera angles, animation speed, route styling, and statistics overlays.
8. User exports a video optimized for Instagram, YouTube, or personal sharing.

Initial target activities:
- Mountain treks and hiking trails
- Road running and cycling
- Parks and urban running routes
- Trail running and outdoor adventures

## 3. Product Roadmap

### V1 — Interactive 3D Route Viewer

Goal: Validate GPS parsing and terrain rendering.

Features:
- Upload FIT/GPX files
- Parse GPS coordinates and elevation
- Display activity route on a 3D terrain map
- Satellite and standard map styles
- Interactive zoom, rotate, pan, and tilt
- Display distance, duration, and elevation gain
- Automatic camera framing of the entire route

Estimated development: 3–5 days.

### V2 — Automated Route Flyover

Goal: Transform a static route into an animated journey.

Features:
- Animated camera following the GPS track
- Progressive route drawing
- Moving position marker
- Adjustable playback speed
- Camera zoom and tilt controls
- Smooth interpolation between GPS points
- Start and finish markers
- Basic animation preview

Estimated additional development: 4–7 days.

### V3 — Cinematic Video Generator

Goal: Generate professional, social-media-ready videos.

Features:
- Automatic cinematic camera movement
- Orbit and aerial flyover transitions
- Dynamic camera altitude and bearing
- Route and elevation statistics overlays
- Custom titles and activity summaries
- Vertical 9:16 and landscape 16:9 exports
- 1080p MP4 rendering
- Background music and optional transitions
- Custom themes and route colors

Estimated additional development: 1–2 weeks.

### V4 — Advanced Adventure Storytelling

Potential future features:
- Garmin Connect integration
- Automatic detection of climbs, summits, and notable segments
- Highlight steep gradients and elevation changes
- Synchronize phone or action-camera footage with GPS timestamps
- AI-assisted cinematic camera planning
- Multiple routes and activity comparisons
- Branded video templates
- Automated adventure recap videos

## 6. Important Technical Principles

Garmin provides activity data, not the surrounding 3D landscape.

GPS data determines where the user traveled.

Terrain elevation datasets determine the geographical landscape.

Map and satellite tiles provide the visual appearance.

The camera engine determines how the journey is presented.

The video rendering pipeline transforms the animation into a downloadable video.

For the MVP, avoid developing a custom 3D engine. Use existing geospatial rendering libraries and focus engineering effort on route visualization, animation quality, and user experience.

## 7. Main Technical Challenges

Terrain Accuracy:
Elevation datasets have limited resolution. Narrow paths, cliffs, rocks, buildings, and vegetation may not be represented accurately.

GPS Quality:
Recorded routes can contain drift, missing points, inaccurate elevation, and irregular timestamps.

Route Alignment:
GPS elevation and terrain elevation can differ. Routes must be positioned correctly above the rendered terrain without visibly floating or clipping through it.

Camera Automation:
Automatic flyovers must handle sharp turns, steep slopes, mountain obstructions, and changes in route direction.

Rendering Performance:
High-resolution terrain, satellite imagery, and camera animations can require significant GPU resources.

Video Export:
Consistent frame capture, tile-loading synchronization, and reliable MP4 generation are more complicated than interactive browser playback.

Data Licensing:
Commercial use and video export rights must be checked for mapping SDKs, elevation datasets, satellite imagery, and other third-party assets.

## 8. MVP Development Strategy

Start with a real Garmin activity, preferably a mountainous trek with significant elevation variation.

Suggested test case:
Mount Rinjani — Senaru Crater Rim trek, Lombok, Indonesia.

Initial implementation sequence:

1. Build a simple FIT/GPX upload interface.
2. Convert uploaded data into normalized GeoJSON.
3. Render the route using Mapbox GL JS.
4. Enable 3D terrain and satellite imagery.
5. Automatically position the camera to display the entire route.
6. Implement route-following animation.
7. Add a playback timeline and basic camera controls.
8. Generate a short test video.
9. Improve camera transitions and terrain presentation.

Initial success criteria:
- A real Garmin route renders accurately.
- The terrain matches the geographical location.
- Users can navigate the 3D map smoothly.
- The flyover follows the actual route.
- A short animation can be exported as MP4.

## 9. Product Differentiation

The initial product overlaps with existing GPS visualization tools, including Strava Flyover and Relive.

The long-term opportunity is not simply another route viewer.

TerrainFly could evolve into an automated outdoor adventure storytelling platform.

Potential differentiators:
- Cinematic-quality 3D animations
- More customizable camera movements
- Automatic summit and climb highlights
- Rich Garmin activity overlays
- GPS-synchronized real-world video
- High-quality social-media templates
- One-click adventure recap generation

## 10. Potential Business Model

Possible future monetization:
- Free interactive 3D route viewer
- Limited free video exports
- Premium high-resolution exports
- Advanced cinematic templates
- Subscription for frequent athletes and creators
- Pay-per-export option

These are hypotheses to validate, not confirmed business requirements.

## 11. Development Cost and Complexity

Estimated engineering difficulty:
- Basic 3D route viewer: 4/10
- Animated flyover: 5–6/10
- Polished cinematic video generator: 7/10

Estimated MVP timeline:
Approximately 1–3 weeks for a solo developer using existing libraries and AI-assisted coding tools.

Potential infrastructure costs:
- Local prototype: potentially free
- Early hosted MVP: approximately $0–50/month, depending on usage
- Production: variable terrain/map API, storage, and video-rendering costs

Development estimates exclude extensive production hardening, commercial licensing costs, and complex video infrastructure.

## 14. Immediate Next Step

Build the simplest possible end-to-end prototype:

Garmin FIT/GPX File
    -> GPS Parsing
    -> 3D Terrain Rendering
    -> Route Overlay
    -> Camera Flyover

Do not begin with authentication, payments, AI-generated storytelling, cloud rendering infrastructure, or automatic Garmin synchronization.

First prove that a real trek can be transformed into an attractive, accurate, interactive 3D flyover.

Once this works, prioritize automatic cinematic camera movement and MP4 export.

## Product Vision

Turn every outdoor activity into a cinematic 3D story.

TerrainFly should make creating an engaging adventure visualization as simple as uploading a GPS activity file.
